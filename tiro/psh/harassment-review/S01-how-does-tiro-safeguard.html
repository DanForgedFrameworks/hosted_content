The 5 Rs

If you are ever worried about anyone else, this is the guidance to follow, in order. Select each step to see what it means.

  1. Step 1Recognise›

    Be alert to signs, things people tell you, changes in behaviour, missed contact, and risks online or at work. You do not need to prove what happened.

  2. Step 2Respond›

    Stay calm. Listen and reassure. Do not promise to keep it secret, do not ask leading questions, and do not investigate.

  3. Step 3Record›

    Write down the facts as soon as you can, in the person's own words: the date, time and place, who was there, what was said or seen, and what you did.

  4. Step 4Report›

    Tell a Designated Safeguarding Lead straight away. Call them, or email safeguarding@tiro.ac.uk.

  5. Step 5Refer›

    The Designated Safeguarding Lead decides whether to refer to social care, Family Help, the police, Prevent, health services or another agency.

What happens when a concern is raised

This is TIRO's process from the Safeguarding and Prevent Policy (version 5.1). Full details are in the policy.

Open the Safeguarding and Prevent Policy (opens in a new tab)

  1. 1. Safeguarding concern identified

    A concern may come from a disclosure, observation, behaviour change, attendance concern, online concern, workplace concern, or information shared by another person.

  2. Question
    2. Is there an immediate risk of harm, medical emergency, crime in progress, or danger now?

    If yes

    UrgentCall 999 immediately

    Then contact a Designated Safeguarding Lead as soon as it is safe to do so.

    If no, go to the next step.

  3. 3. Respond appropriately

    Stay calm. Listen. Reassure. Do not promise secrecy. Do not investigate. Use open prompts where needed.

  4. 4. Record the concern

    Record the facts as soon as possible. Use the learner's own words where possible. Include dates, times, what was seen or heard, who was present, and any action already taken.

  5. 5. Report to TIRO

    Contact a Designated Safeguarding Lead immediately by phone where there is risk, or email safeguarding@tiro.ac.uk.

  6. 6. The Designated Safeguarding Lead reviews the concern

    The Designated Safeguarding Lead considers risk, checks relevant information, records action taken, and decides next steps.

  7. Question
    7. Is an external referral or urgent action needed?

    If yes

    The Designated Safeguarding Lead refers to the appropriate agency

    This may include children's social care, adult social care, police, Prevent or Channel, health services, emergency services, or another relevant agency.

    If no, go to the next step.

  8. 8. The Designated Safeguarding Lead records the reasons and agrees internal support or monitoring

    This may include learner support, employer engagement, wellbeing check-ins, signposting, review actions, or continued monitoring.

  9. 9. The concern stays open until the right action is complete

    The Designated Safeguarding Lead keeps the case under review, records updates, and closes the concern when appropriate.

If you think the right action has not been taken

If you believe a learner is still at risk, escalate the concern to another Designated Safeguarding Lead, the Safeguarding Governor or the CEO, or make a referral directly to the appropriate external agency.

Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility.

Our commitment to you
  • TIRO is committed to safeguarding and promoting the welfare of all learners, including children, young people and adults at risk.
  • Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility, and TIRO will always act in the learner's best interests.
  • TIRO listens to learners and takes account of their wishes and feelings where it is safe and appropriate to do so. The policy applies to all TIRO learners, staff, governors and contractors, and to the employers and partners who work with TIRO learners.
