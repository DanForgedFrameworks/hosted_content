Sexual harassment: what it is and what to do

Urgent In an emergency

If you or anyone you know is in immediate danger, a threat to life or at risk from criminal activities, do not hesitate to ring 999.

Call 999

Then contact a Designated Safeguarding Lead as soon as it is safe to do so.

Sexual harassment has no place at TIRO, at your workplace or online. If it happens to you, or you see it happen to someone else, tell us. You do not need evidence.

What counts as sexual harassment

Sexual harassment is unwanted behaviour of a sexual nature that:

  • violates your dignity, or
  • makes you feel intimidated, degraded, humiliated or offended, or creates a hostile atmosphere.
  • It does not have to be deliberate. What matters is the effect on you, or on anyone who sees it.
  • One incident can be enough.
  • It can happen between people of any gender, in person, online or by message.

Important "It was only banter" is not an excuse

Saying it was banter, a joke or a compliment, or "I didn't mean it like that", does not make it acceptable. Behaviour is judged by its impact, not just what someone meant.

Examples

These are some examples. Something can still be sexual harassment if it is not listed here.

  • Things people say
    • Sexual comments or jokes
    • Suggestive remarks
    • Questions about your sex life
    • Catcalling
    • Sexual propositions
  • Things people do
    • Unwanted touching, hugs or kissing
    • Standing unnecessarily close
    • Blocking your way
    • Sexual assault
  • Things people show
    • Pornographic material or sexual images
    • Sexual gestures
    • Leering or staring
  • Online and on your phone
    • Sexual messages
    • Sharing sexual images, including images made with AI
    • Online harassment
    • Inappropriate contact on social media
    • Offensive memes
  • Using power or position
    • Asking for sexual favours
    • Offering opportunities in return for a relationship
    • Favouritism because of a relationship

Who it can come from

Anyone. That includes TIRO staff, your employer or manager, people you work with, customers, clients, suppliers, contractors, visitors and members of the public. You should never feel you have to put up with it to protect a work relationship.

TIRO staff must keep professional boundaries

Anyone working for TIRO must never:

  • use their position to seek a personal, emotional or sexual relationship with you
  • share their personal social media accounts with you
  • swap personal contact details with you outside approved channels

If a member of TIRO staff does any of these, tell a Designated Safeguarding Lead. If it is about a Safeguarding Lead, contact the Safeguarding Governor or the CEO. Their details are in How does TIRO safeguard?

If it happens at your workplace

As an apprentice, you spend most of your time with your employer. If something happens there:

  • Tell us as soon as you can.
  • TIRO will support you, wherever it happened.
  • A Designated Safeguarding Lead may be involved.
  • TIRO may talk to your employer, where that is appropriate.
  • TIRO may arrange things differently to keep you safe.

Your safety and wellbeing always come first.

If you see it happen

Comments and jokes that nobody challenges can grow into more serious behaviour. Speaking up early helps stop that.

  • If it feels safe to speak up

    You can keep it simple. For example:

    • "That's not okay."
    • "What do you mean by that?"
  • If it does not feel safe

    You are never expected to put yourself at risk. Instead:

    • check in with the person it happened to
    • encourage them to get support
    • report what you saw

How to report it

Tell any of these. Use whichever you are most comfortable with. You do not have to deal with it yourself, and you are never expected to confront the person.

  • Option 1
    Your Work-based Learning Coach

    Your main point of contact for your apprenticeship.

  • Option 2
    The Safeguarding inbox

    Your email goes to all of the Safeguarding Leads at once.

    safeguarding@tiro.ac.uk

  • Option 3
    A Designated Safeguarding Lead

    Call or email any of them directly.

    See their details

You will not be treated unfairly for speaking up

TIRO does not tolerate anyone being treated badly for raising a concern, supporting someone who does, or helping with an investigation. Reports are handled promptly, respectfully, sensitively and as confidentially as possible.

What happens next

  1. You raise a concern. You will be listened to and taken seriously.
  2. TIRO checks for any immediate risk, considers safeguarding and works out what support you need. Temporary arrangements may be put in place to keep people safe.
  3. If more is needed, an impartial person looks into it, fairly and sensitively.
  4. There is an outcome. It could be anything from no further action to disciplinary action, a safeguarding referral or a referral to the police. You will be told the outcome where that is appropriate and allowed.
  5. Support can carry on afterwards, including wellbeing support and pointers to specialist organisations.

Want to understand it better?

HASSL is a UK campaign against harassment. Its short films, including Girls Just Wanna and Contradictions, show what harassment looks like day to day and the pressure it puts on people.

HASSL raises awareness. It does not run a helpline, so if you need support, use the services below.
