How does your team choose and explain a test?

Three short writing prompts. Write your own answer first, then reveal a model answer to compare. At the end you can copy or download your summary.

The scenario

A team planning a patient's next steps

Your team is planning the next steps for a patient with advanced nonsquamous lung cancer. A colleague reminds everyone of this statement.

Every patient with advanced nonsquamous lung cancer should have EGFR testing at diagnosis, as part of broad biomarker testing.

Think about your own team as you answer. There are no right or wrong answers, only your own practice.

Prompt 1 of 3
Available once you have written something.

Model answer. The pulmonologist or radiologist aims to take enough tissue for diagnosis and molecular testing. The pathologist saves tissue and, where policy allows, sends it automatically (reflex testing). The oncologist checks results are back before starting treatment.

Compare: did you name each role and say what that person does? Add anything you missed.

Prompt 2 of 3
Available once you have written something.

Model answer. A blood test looks for tumor DNA in the bloodstream (ctDNA).

Compare: a clear explanation uses short, everyday words, says what the test is for, and tells the patient what happens next. It also leaves room for the patient to ask a question.

Prompt 3 of 3
Available once you have written something.

Model answer. I will ask each team member, at the start of the discussion, what they need from the others to do their part, and I will check with the patient at the end of the conversation that they can say back what happens next.

Compare: a useful change is small, specific and something you can do within the next week.
